Fall Deer Rut Increases Crash Risk for Wisconsin Motorists

Safety Strategies Posted on October 15, 2026

As Wisconsin enters the fall deer mating season, motorists are reminded to remain alert and use extra caution while traveling on local roads and highways. During the annual fall rut, deer become more active and frequently move across roadways, increasing the risk of vehicle crashes.

The Menomonee Falls Police Department is urging drivers to be especially vigilant during the fall months, when deer-related crashes traditionally reach their highest levels.

According to the Wisconsin Department of Transportation (DOT), in 2025, approximately 42% of all deer-related crashes in Wisconsin occurred between October and December. November was the most dangerous month, with 3,389 reported crashes involving deer in a single month. 

Deer Crashes Across Wisconsin

In 2025, Wisconsin recorded 17,793 crashes involving deer, resulting in six fatalities and 720 injuries.

The following counties reported the highest numbers of deer-related crashes:

  • Waukesha County: 1,010
  • Washington County: 857
  • Sheboygan County: 830
  • Dane County: 792
  • St. Croix County: 769
  • Manitowoc County: 711
  • Fond du Lac County: 704
  • Walworth County: 642
  • Outagamie County: 590
  • Winnebago County: 566

With Waukesha County reporting the highest number of deer-related crashes statewide, motorists traveling throughout Menomonee Falls and the surrounding communities should remain particularly alert, especially during early morning and evening commutes.

Tips to Reduce Your Risk of a Deer Crash

Deer can be active at any time, but they are generally most active around dawn and dusk. Reduced visibility, changing weather conditions, and increased deer movement make these periods especially hazardous.

Motorists should follow these precautions:

  • Slow down and remain alert. Reduce speed when traveling through wooded areas, near fields, along rural roads and wherever deer-warning signs are posted.
  • Use your headlights appropriately. Use low-beam headlights when following another vehicle or approaching oncoming traffic. High beams can improve visibility when the road is clear, and when their use is appropriate.
  • Scan beyond the roadway. Watch roadsides, ditches, and field edges for movement or reflective eyes. Avoid focusing exclusively on the vehicle directly ahead.
  • Expect additional deer. Deer often travel in groups. If one crosses the road, slow down and watch for others following behind.
  • Avoid distractions. Put away your phone, maintain a safe following distance, and keep your attention on the roadway.
  • Always wear your seat belt. Proper seat belt use reduces the risk of serious injury or death if a collision occurs.
  • Do not rely on deer whistles or other deterrents. These devices should not replace attentive driving, appropriate speed or safe braking.


What to Do if a Deer Enters Your Path

Deer can behave unpredictably. They may stop suddenly, reverse direction, or move directly into a vehicle’s path.

If a collision appears unavoidable, brake firmly while maintaining control of your vehicle and staying in your lane whenever possible. Swerving to avoid a deer can cause a driver to lose control, leave the roadway, or collide with another vehicle or a fixed object.

If you see a deer near the road, reduce your speed and be prepared to stop. Do not accelerate in an attempt to pass in front of it.

Motorcyclists should exercise additional caution because a collision with a deer can cause a rider to lose control or be thrown from the motorcycle. In 2025, Wisconsin recorded 223 deer-related motorcycle crashes, resulting in four fatalities and 80 serious injuries. Approximately 38% of those crashes resulted in a fatality or serious injury.

Motorcyclists should reduce speed, maintain control, and apply controlled braking. An evasive maneuver should only be attempted when it can be performed safely without creating a greater hazard.

What to Do After a Deer Collision

If you strike a deer, prioritize safety and follow these steps:

  1. Move to a safe location. If possible, move your vehicle out of traffic and onto the shoulder or another safe area. Activate your hazard lights.
  2. Call 911 when necessary. Request emergency assistance if anyone is injured, the roadway is obstructed, or the crash presents an immediate traffic hazard.
  3. Remain in your vehicle when it is safe to do so. Walking along a roadway can expose you to additional danger. If your vehicle is in an unsafe location, use your judgment and move to a safer area if you can do so without entering traffic.
  4. Do not approach an injured deer. An injured animal may kick, struggle, or behave unpredictably.
  5. Contact law enforcement when appropriate. If there are no injuries and no immediate hazard, contact the appropriate law enforcement agency to determine whether a report is necessary.
  6. Report the crash and document the damage. Follow law enforcement guidance, exchange information if another vehicle is involved, and photograph vehicle damage when it is safe.
  7. Arrange for deer removal if necessary. Contact the appropriate county highway department or sheriff’s department non-emergency line regarding a deer carcass on or alongside the roadway.


Stay Alert in Menomonee Falls

Motorists traveling in Menomonee Falls should be especially attentive along wooded roadways, near parks and trails, and on roads bordering open fields or undeveloped property. Deer can also enter busy suburban streets, so drivers should not assume that a well-lit or heavily traveled road is free from risk.

Extra caution is warranted during the morning and evening commute, particularly when visibility is reduced by darkness, rain, fog or glare.

As the fall season continues, slowing down, scanning the roadway and remaining prepared to stop can help prevent deer-related crashes and protect motorists, passengers and other road users.

Drive cautiously, stay alert and remember: if you see one deer, expect the possibility of another.
