East Lansing Fire Department Offers Safety Tips for Thanksgiving Cooking

News Releases Posted on November 24, 2026

EAST LANSING, Mich. — The East Lansing Fire Department (ELFD), along with the National Fire Protection Association (NFPA), has offered tips for community members to stay safe in the kitchen this holiday season.

Thanksgiving is the peak day of the year for home cooking fires in the U.S., according to the NFPA. An estimated 1,139 home cooking fires were reported to U.S fire departments on Thanksgiving Day in 2024, reflecting a 321% increase over the daily average. Christmas Day and Christmas Eve ranked as the second- and third-leading days of the year for cooking fires, respectively.

On average from 2020-2024, 80% of all fires reported on Thanksgiving involved cooking equipment.

Overall, cooking causes roughly half (48%) of all reported U.S. home fires and nearly two of every five (35%) home fire injuries. It is also a leading cause of home fire deaths (16%). Unattended cooking is the leading contributing factor in home cooking fires and fire fatalities.

  • Never leave the kitchen while cooking on the stovetop. Some types of cooking, especially those that involve frying or sautéing with oil, need continuous attention.
  • When cooking a turkey, remain at home and check it regularly.
  • Make use of timers to keep track of cooking times, particularly for foods that require longer cook times.
  • Keep things that can catch fire, such as oven mitts, wooden utensils, food wrappers and towels, at least 3 feet away from the cooking area.
  • Avoid long sleeves and hanging fabrics that can come in contact with a heat source.
  • Always cook with a lid beside the pan. If a small grease fire starts, smother the flames by sliding the lid over the pan, then turn off the burner. Do not remove the cover because the fire could start again. Let the pan cool.
  • For an oven fire, turn off the heat and keep the door closed. Only open the door once certain the fire is completely out, standing to the side as the door is opened. If there are any doubts or concerns, contact the fire department for assistance.
  • Keep children and pets at least 3 feet away from the stove and areas where hot foods or drinks are prepared or carried, as the steam or splash could cause serious burns. Hot foods and liquids should be placed away from the edge of tables and counters.
  • Keep the floor clear to prevent tripping hazards.
  • Make sure electric cords are not dangling from the counter.
  • Test smoke alarms to ensure they are working properly.

Additionally, the NFPA strongly discourages the use of turkey fryers that use cooking oil, which can cause devastating burns. For a safer alternative, use an infrared fryer, air fryer or electric turkey fryer which mimics the taste of an oil-fried turkey, or consider purchasing a fried turkey from a grocery store or restaurant.

For more tips on how to stay safe throughout the year, visit www.cityofeastlansing.com/SafetyTips.
