[Ord. No. 932 §10.1231, 11-3-1980; Ord. No. 24-05, 3-19-2024]
A. 
Definitions. As used in this Section, the following terms shall have these prescribed meanings:
OPEN BURNING
The burning of any materials wherein air contaminants resulting from combustion are emitted directly into the ambient air without passing through a stack or chimney from an enclosed chamber. For the purposes of this definition, a chamber shall be regarded as enclosed when, during the time combustion takes place, only such apertures, ducts, stacks, flues or chimneys as are necessary to provide combustion air and to permit the escape of exhaust gases are open.
YARD WASTE
Trees, tree leaves, pine straw, grass clippings, brush or other vegetation that originated on the property where the burning is to occur.
B. 
Open burning permitted by this Section shall be subject to the following restrictions:
1. 
The burning of yard waste is permitted but must be done at least twenty (20) feet from any other structure and at all times be monitored by a competent person capable of containing or extinguishing fire if necessary.
2. 
The burning of paper, garbage, household refuse, tires, construction materials, any item coated with rubber or plastic and any other kinds of waste is prohibited.
3. 
If climatological conditions such as wind, drought, humidity or other similar conditions indicate that open burning may create a hazard to the public; the Mayor, or designee, may invoke a temporary ban on open burning.
4. 
Under no circumstance shall open burning be allowed as part of a salvage operation or recycling activities.
C. 
Nothing in the Section shall be construed to prohibit or restrict the following:
1. 
Fireplaces or wood burning stoves located wholly within a residence.
2. 
Outdoor barbecues for the preparation of food, recreational or ceremonial fires if properly supervised.
a. 
Fuel for recreational or ceremonial fires shall only consist of natural wood, manufactured fire log or other appropriate material not otherwise prohibited by this Section.
D. 
No fires, regardless of type, shall either be started or allowed to continue burning when wind speed or direction will cause embers or other burning material to be carried onto any building, other combustible material or a neighboring property; nor at any time that the direction of the wind will carry smoke into the windows of a neighboring building.
E. 
No fire, of any type or material, shall cause a public nuisance. Any release of noxious smoke, offensive odor or smoke of sufficient quantity or quality so as to inhibit the use and enjoyment of neighboring property is hereby declared a nuisance.
F. 
Any person or persons found in violation of this Section shall be deemed guilty of a misdemeanor and upon conviction shall be punished by a fine of not more than five hundred dollars ($500.00) or thirty (30) days in jail. Each violation upon each occurrence shall constitute a separate offense.
