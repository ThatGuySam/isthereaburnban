No Open Burning
Open burning is prohibited throughout the city of Coralville. Do not burn trash, yard waste, recycling, or other similar materials.

The Coralville Parks Division occasionally conducts prescribed fire, also known as controlled burns, which are carefully planned and follow strict safety rules. Learn more about controlled burns.

Outdoor Fire Pits & Fireplaces
The only open burning allowed in Coralville are recreational fires using either:
  • Purchased outdoor fire pits or fireplaces constructed of steel, brick, or masonry, and used following the manufacturer’s instructions. 
  • A built-in-place outdoor fire pit or fireplace pre-approved by the Fire Chief or their designee, and constructed of steel, brick, or masonry.
Requirements
When using an outdoor fire pit or fireplace, follow these requirements at all times:
  • Smell, smoke, and hazards: Using an outdoor fire pit or outdoor fireplace is prohibited when it is offensive or objectionable because of smoke or odor, or when the weather or other conditions make such fires hazardous. 
  • Placement: Do not operate an outdoor fire pit/fireplace within 15 feet of a structure or combustible material. 
  • Wood: Only burn dry firewood or commercial logs.
  • Lid: Outdoor fires must be completely contained by wire mesh at all times, including a wire mesh cover or lid to prevent flying embers. The wire mesh must have openings no more than a ¼ inch. The lid or mesh cover must be in place unless wood is being added to the fire. 
  • Adult: At least one adult must be present and attend the fire at all times until the fire is extinguished. 
  • Fire extinguisher: Keep at least one portable fire extinguisher (with a minimum 4-A rating) or other approved fire-extinguishing equipment (such as dirt, sand, water barrel, or garden hose) on site and available for immediate use. 
  • Keep children and pets away and maintain a safe distance from lawn games and foot traffic.
  • Never discard hot coals into anything except a closed, non-combustible container.
  • Never use a gas/propane, wood, or charcoal firepit indoors or in an enclosed space, like a garage or screened-in porch.
Consider Your Neighbors
Before using any outdoor fire pit or fireplace, be mindful of how your outdoor fire impacts neighbors, pets, and the environment:
  • The smoke from a wood outdoor fire pit or fireplace can travel well beyond your property line and into your neighborhood. The smell may travel through open windows and garage doors in your neighbors' homes.
  • Using outdoor fireplaces or fire pits is prohibited when the weather or other conditions make fires hazardous, or objectionable because of smoke or odor.
  • A wood fire releases fine particulate pollution into the air we share. This can make it difficult for people to breathe (especially for those with certain health conditions or respiratory problems). 
  • If you love a firepit but don't want smoke or odor, consider a natural gas or propane firepit.

Commercial Buildings
Contact the fire department for additional rules if the fire pit is located at a commercial building.

Grills
When it comes to open flame cooking devices, the following rules are requirements for apartments, condos, and townhouses, and good practice for one and two-family dwellings:
  • Charcoal burners and other open-flame cooking devices shall not be operated on wood decks or other combustible balconies.
  • Do not grill within 10 feet of combustible construction, such as siding, deck railings, eaves, and overhanging branches.
Exceptions: 
  • Where buildings, balconies, and decks are protected by an automatic sprinkler system. 
  • LP-gas cooking devices having an LP-gas container with a water capacity not greater than 2.5 pounds [nominal 1 pound LP-gas capacity]

Report a Concern
If you feel someone is not following these rules, call the Coralville Fire Department at 319.248.1835 during normal business hours.
