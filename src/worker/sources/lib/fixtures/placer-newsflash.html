Burn Permit Suspension Lifted Nov. 3

Fire Posted on November 02, 2026

CAL FIRE Nevada-Yuba-Placer Unit Chief Jim Hudson has formally canceled the burn permit suspension. Residents with current and valid agricultural and residential burn permits may resume burning on permissive burn days.

Agricultural burns must be inspected by CAL FIRE before burning through the end of peak fire season. Inspections may also be required for other types of burns. Contact the Placer County Air Pollution Control District to verify whether an inspection is required.

Check burn day status

Residents must verify that it is a permissive burn day before burning by contacting the Placer County Air Pollution Control District:

  • Auburn area, including all cellphone users: 530-889-6868
  • Outside Auburn, toll-free: 800-998-BURN (2876)
  • Check Placer County burn day status

CAL FIRE burn permits will continue to be required until cooler temperatures, higher humidity and the chance of winter weather reduce the threat of wildfire.

Property owners and residents are urged to use caution when conducting debris or agricultural burns, follow all applicable guidelines and maintain control of their fires at all times. Individuals may be held civilly and/or criminally liable if a fire escapes their control or spreads to neighboring property.

Pile burning requirements

When conducting residential pile burns, follow these safety requirements:

  • Only dry, natural vegetative material, such as leaves, pine needles and tree trimmings, may be burned.
  • Burning trash, milled lumber or other debris is prohibited.
  • Do not burn on windy days.
  • Piles should be no larger than 4 feet in diameter and height. Additional material may be added as the pile burns down.
  • Clear a 10-foot area around the pile down to bare soil.
  • Keep a shovel and water source nearby.
  • An adult must remain in attendance at all times.

Safe residential pile burning of forest residue by landowners is an important tool for reducing fire hazards. State, federal and local land management and fire agencies will also use this opportunity to conduct prescribed burns to improve forest health and resiliency on private and public lands.

For more information about burning, visit CAL FIRE.
