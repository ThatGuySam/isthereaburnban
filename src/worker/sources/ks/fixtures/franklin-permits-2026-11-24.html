Burn Permits

Franklin County requires a burn permit for most open burning conducted in the unincorporated areas of Franklin County.

Burn permits issued by Franklin County do not apply inside incorporated city limits. If you live within a city, contact your city for its burning requirements.

Residents within the City of Ottawa should contact the Ottawa Fire Department at 785-229-3700 for burning information.

Before You Burn

Before conducting an open burn:

  • Obtain the required burn permit.

  • Check whether burning is allowed that day.

  • Check current and expected weather conditions.

  • Make sure adequate personnel, equipment, and water are available to control the fire.

  • Consider surrounding vegetation, structures, roads, utilities, and other hazards.

  • Notify occupants of an occupied dwelling within 1,000 feet of the burn.

  • Follow all permit conditions and instructions from the local fire authority.

Never burn when a Burn Ban or No Burn Order is in effect.

Burn Permit Conditions

Franklin County burn permits are subject to specific conditions designed to reduce the risk of uncontrolled fire and protect public safety.

Important conditions include:

  • Surface winds must be at least 5 mph and no greater than 15 mph.

  • New burning may not begin during the period beginning two hours before sunset and ending one hour after sunrise.

  • Material may not be added to a fire beginning two hours before sunset.

  • Fires must be attended and reasonable precautions taken to prevent the fire from spreading.

  • Burning may not create a traffic or other safety hazard.

  • Heavy smoke-producing materials such as petroleum products, oils, tires, composition shingles, and tar paper may not be burned.

  • Additional requirements may apply depending on the location or type of burn.

Anyone conducting an open burn is responsible for complying with all applicable Franklin County requirements, Kansas open-burning regulations, and instructions from the local fire authority.

Burn Bans and No Burn Orders

A burn permit does not guarantee that burning will be allowed.

Burning may be prohibited when weather conditions, vegetation, fire danger, or other circumstances create an unacceptable risk.

When a Burn Ban or No Burn Order is in effect:

  • Do not conduct open burning.

  • Previously issued permits do not authorize burning during the restriction.

  • The online burn permit system may be unavailable.

For current burning status, call 785-229-3501.

If a Fire Gets Out of Control

If your fire escapes or becomes unsafe, call 911 immediately.

Do not delay requesting assistance while attempting to control a fire that is spreading beyond your ability to safely contain it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit for a campfire or ceremonial fire?

Small campfires and ceremonial fires may be exempt from the burn permit requirement when they meet applicable requirements. Burn bans and other fire restrictions still apply.

My brush pile is still burning the next day. Do I need another permit?

A new permit may not be required simply because a large brush pile continues to burn. You remain responsible for monitoring the fire, complying with permit conditions, and preventing the fire from spreading.

What if I plan to burn for several days?

Check burning conditions and restrictions each day. Active burning on additional days may require an additional permit.

How do I know if burning is allowed today?

Check the online burn permit system. The system may be unavailable when burning is prohibited.

You may also call 785-229-3501 for current burning status.

What if my fire gets out of control?

Call 911 immediately. The appropriate fire department will be dispatched.
