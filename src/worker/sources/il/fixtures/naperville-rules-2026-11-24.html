Open Burning Permits

Open burning is largely prohibited within Naperville's city limits, with some limited exceptions when the Naperville Fire Department may issue permits for certain open burns, including campfires, cookouts, bonfires and prairie burns.

Important to Know

Recreational Fires

Recreational fires, which include fires used for cooking and in commercially built outdoor fireplaces, do not require an open burn permit. Only charcoal and clean, dry wood may be burned; garbage and trash cannot be.

Timeline

Submit permit applications one week before the burn date.

Additional Regulations

Additional regulations regarding this type of permit are detailed in the City's Municipal Codebook in 4-1-5: Open Burning, 5-2D-4: Prohibited, Acts, Conditions and 4-1-4: Dense Smoke.

Apply for a Permit

Apply for an open burn permit by filling out a Naperville Fire Prescribed Burn Application (PDF) or a Naperville Fire Recreational Burn Application (PDF).

Note: For prairie grass (prescribed) burns, to obtain an open burn permit from the Fire Department, first secure an Illinois EPA Open Burning Permit and bring it to the Fire Department at 1380 Aurora Avenue.

Location-Specific Regulations

  1. Naperville City Limits & Naperville Fire Protection District

Permitted Instances

In the City of Naperville and Naperville Fire Protection District, uncontrolled open burning (leaves, construction debris and garbage) is prohibited except for the following when an Open Burn Permit is issued by the Fire Prevention Bureau:

  • Prairie grass burns (which require an IEPA permit)
  • Bonfires
  • Cookouts and campfires (sometimes)

The Fire Department Operations Assistant Chief and Emergency Communications Center receive copies of the Open Burn Permit. Permit applications must be submitted one week before the burn date.

Other Regulations

Plant Material
It shall be unlawful and a nuisance for any person to burn or cause to be burned anywhere in the City any leaves, branches, or similar foliage from trees, shrubs, bushes, or plants.

Open Burning
No person shall make, kindle, or light any fire in any part of any of the public streets, alleys, or other public places in the City, except an employee of the City in the line of duty; nor upon any private property if there be apparent danger of fire to any buildings or property in the neighborhood of the same, without some person being in continuous attendance at such fire who is capable of preventing the spreading thereof.

Dense Smoke
It shall be unlawful to cause or permit the emission of dense smoke from any fire, chimney, engine, oil burner, or any other agency in the City so as to cause annoyance or discomfort to the resident thereof.

Location
No person shall kindle or maintain any bonfire, campfire, recreational, or cooking fire on any private land unless the location is not less than 50 feet from any structure. Fires in approved containers shall be permitted within 15 feet of any structure.

Complaint Response

When responding to an open burning complaint dispatched by Naperville's Emergency Communications Center (ECC), NFD units shall respond in non-emergency mode.

If properly permitted, the permit holder or other responsible party shall be present during open burns. Any open burn or fire out of control and/or presenting a dangerous situation, or that produces smoke which may be the cause of a reported health problem, shall be extinguished. This includes recreational fires. 

If necessary, the appropriate law enforcement agency may be summoned to the scene to enforce any open burning ordinances. Repeated responses and violations of the open burning regulations at the same location shall be reported to the Fire Prevention Bureau for follow-up action.

The Fire Department unit assigned to the open burning incident shall attempt to contact the complainant from the station after the incident and report any action taken.

  1. Unincorporated DuPage County

If you live in unincorporated DuPage County, open burning is prohibited with certain limited exceptions. Burning construction debris is illegal.

  1. Unincorporated Will County

If you live in unincorporated Will County, open burning is prohibited with limited exceptions. It is illegal to burn construction debris in Will County.

Plant Material

The burning of dry leaves, branches, or bushes originating on an owner's property is permitted. This applies only to dry landscape waste burned on the property of origin.

Such open burning is allowed under the following conditions:

  1. Fires (including legitimate campfires) shall not be located less than 50 feet from any property line or any structure, with adequate provisions made to prevent the spread of the fire.
  2. Fires in approved containers shall be located not less than 15 feet from any neighboring structure.
  3. A competent person shall constantly attend all such fires until the fire is extinguished. This person shall have a hose connected to a water supply or other fire extinguishing equipment ready for use. This person shall be responsible for notifying the local fire department before starting the fire.
