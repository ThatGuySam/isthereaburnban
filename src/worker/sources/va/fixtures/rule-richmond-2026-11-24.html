Open Burning

  • Open burning is permitted only with approval from the Fire Code Official and must meet all conditions specified for silvicultural, recreational, religious, ceremonial, trench, and bonfire activities.

  • Open burning that is offensive or objectionable due to smoke emissions, or that becomes hazardous because of atmospheric conditions or local circumstances, is strictly prohibited.

  • The use of burn barrels is not prohibited; however, they must comply with the same guidelines that apply to fire pits.

  • Burning on any property without ownership or written permission from the property owner is always prohibited.

  • The Fire Department has the authority to extinguish any fire—permitted or not—that creates or contributes to a hazardous or objectionable situation. Repeated calls for service to the same location may result in the fire being extinguished.

  • To determine if a permit is required, please contact the Fire Marshal’s Office at (804) 646-6640.

 

Portable Outdoor Fire Devices

  • Stoves, incinerators, and other controlled-burning devices—including patio fireplaces—are not classified as open burning and therefore do not require an open burning permit.

  • Pre-manufactured fireplace devices must be placed at least 15 feet away from any structure or combustible material. As with recreational fires, you must have an approved means of extinguishment readily available.

  • As with any heat-producing device, patio fireplaces should be used with caution, keeping them safely distanced from buildings and combustible materials, and maintaining constant supervision while in use.

  • The Richmond Fire Department recommends using these devices strictly in accordance with all manufacturer instructions and safety guidelines.

  • Usage of these devices on any property without ownership or written permission from the property owner is always prohibited.
