The James City County Fire Department and Fire Marshal plays a crucial role in regulating and authorizing various activities related to fire use and management. Specifically, individuals or organizations intending to engage in activities such as bonfires, open burning of debris waste, and agricultural/forest management burns are required to obtain permits from the Fire Department. These permits serve as official approval for the execution of these fire-related activities but are subject to adherence to specific guidelines. 

The issuance of permits ensures that safety measures are in place, minimizing the risk of uncontrolled fires and potential hazards to both property and lives. The guidelines established by the federal, state, and local governments are designed to balance facilitating these activities and safeguarding the community and the environment. They may include restrictions on the size and location of bonfires, the types of materials allowed for open burning, and the proper techniques for conducting agricultural/forest management burns.

By obtaining a permit, individuals or entities commit to following these guidelines and, in turn, contribute to the overall safety and well-being of the community. The Fire Department's involvement in the permitting process defines our responsibility to regulate fire-related activities effectively.
