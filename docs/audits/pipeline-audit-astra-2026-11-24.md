**What holds up.** With the configured single queue consumer, source payload/head changes are batched, publication pauses source updates while staging, and each state revision, transitions and pending notices are one D1 transaction. Readers pin the catalog and check shard revisions. National fallback checks it received every required fragment. Ordinary eventual consistency can return an older coherent edition. During a read failure, multiple revisions of lag or an unfinished alert run there are different guarantees, described above. [KV consistency](https://developers.cloudflare.com/kv/concepts/how-kv-works/)

**Capacity.** At the first check the local registry had 209 sources, 19 hourly, 129 on local-time schedules and 61 manual. Summing the scheduled hours gave 713 nominal polls/day, before retries and publication jobs. The 84 entries/465 polls in `docs/fetchers.md:103` describe an older registry. Beta exposed 178 source records, including six healthy full feeds, 102 healthy positive-only, nine unsuccessful positive-only and 61 manual. The local worktree was changing. Paid Workers default to 30 s HTTP CPU with a configurable maximum of 300 s, 10,000 subrequests by default, 128 MB per isolate and six connections waiting for headers. An hourly scheduled handler and a queue consumer have different limits. The hourly cron has up to 15 minutes of CPU. Queue wall time is 15 minutes, with configurable CPU up to five minutes. D1 allows 1,000 queries per Paid invocation. Measure Worker CPU, queue age, provider latency, encoded bytes and D1 work before increasing limits. The 32-state write cap and custom pretty-JSON assembly merit re-evaluation first.
